What we did with the debt figures (2026-08-25)

1. Text incorporating the new debt panels of Figures 5 and 6

The debt results were woven through the whole paper, not only Section 5. The
abstract closes with a debt sentence covering both reforms (numbers for
reallocation, qualitative for efficiency, mirroring the output treatment). The
introduction's findings paragraphs carry the debt results for reallocation and
efficiency; the reallocation results were restructured into two paragraphs,
with public R&D given its own sentence before the complementarity result. The
Section 5 intro states that experiments are evaluated by output and debt and
defines the debt measure. Sections 5.1 and 5.2 report the magnitudes per
instrument. The conclusions state that both reforms lower debt and that growth
and debt containment are complements, with the causal clause covering both the
budget-neutral reallocations and the costless efficiency improvements.
Subsection 5.3 (human capital + R&D mix) deliberately stays output-only.

2. New appendix with detailed transmission charts

A new final appendix, "Detailed Responses to the Policy Experiments," shows two
5x4 grids in the layout of Figure 2: the three AE reallocation experiments of
Figure 5, and the three standard AE efficiency experiments of Figure 6, panel a
(reallocation plus 25-year closure of the calibrated gaps). Colors match
Figure 5. Generated by pyScripts/2026-06_wp-imf/plotPolicyExperimentIRFs.py,
registered in chartTable.csv and the invoke task runner.

3. Debt reported as the true ratio change, in every figure

Previously all debt panels showed (experiment debt - steady-state debt) /
steady-state annual GDP, the numerator effect only. Now every figure reports
the change in the measured debt-to-GDP ratio, with debt divided by current
annual final demand: (bbar + delta) / (1 + yd) - bbar, using the calibrated
steady-state ratios (78.8 percent AE, 46.4 percent EMDE). The conversion lives
in wp_charts.debt_to_ratio_change and is applied by all seven debt-plotting
scripts; no model re-runs were needed. The primary deficit and transfers stay
on steady-state-GDP scaling (fiscal-impulse reading; corrections negligible).
This also settles the reporting half of the old TODO about normalizing debt by
final demand rather than gross output; only the model-internal rule variable
by = b/y remains for a future re-run.

4. Consequences for the headline numbers (2050)

The denominator effect adds roughly one-third to one-half. Reallocation:
infrastructure about 7 percentage points in both economies (was 5), human
capital about 8 (was 5), AE R&D about 10 (was 7.5). Additional efficiency
effects: AEs 3 for infrastructure and 1-2 for human capital and R&D; EMDEs 6
and 2 at the 25-year pace, deepening to about 9 and 3 with 15-year closure.
Abstract range now "about 7 to 10 percentage points."

5. Clarity for the reader

Every debt-showing figure note states the convention in identical wording
("...the change in the debt-to-GDP ratio in percentage points, with debt
measured against current annual GDP"). The Section 5 intro explains that the
measure includes the denominator contribution of higher output, and the
conclusions credit both the revenue gains and the larger GDP base. A final
consistency read verified all quoted numbers against the regenerated figure
CSVs and confirmed the Section 4 qualitative claims still hold under the new
measure (debt still accumulates sharply under debt-financed shocks; R&D still
ends lowest; human capital's fall still "modest through 2040").
