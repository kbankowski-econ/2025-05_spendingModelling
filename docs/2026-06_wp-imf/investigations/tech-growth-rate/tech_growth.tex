\noindent
\textbf{Finding.} The model's technology block is detrended by
\texttt{(1+gammaa)}, not by the output growth rate $g$. This note records why
the two growth factors differ when public infrastructure is a productive capital
input.

\section{$\gamma^{a}$ is derived from $g$}

\texttt{gammaa} is not a free parameter. The code sets
\begin{equation}
    \texttt{gammaa = g\^{}((1-alpha-alphaG)/(vartheta-1)) - 1},
    \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
    1+\gamma^{a}=g^{(1-\alpha-\alpha_G)/(\vartheta-1)}.
\end{equation}
With $\alpha=0.3$ and $\vartheta=1.35$, the exponent is about $1.85$ for
AEs, where $\alpha_G=0.054$, and $1.71$ for EMDEs, where $\alpha_G=0.10$.
The corresponding quarterly technology-growth factors are about $1.0086$ and
$1.0216$, respectively.

\section{Balanced-growth restriction}

Technology enters production through the love-of-variety term
$A_{t-1}^{\vartheta-1}$:
\begin{equation}
    Y_t=A_{t-1}^{\vartheta-1}K_{t-1}^{\alpha}
        \left(K^{GI}_{t-1}\right)^{\alpha_G}N_t^{1-\alpha-\alpha_G}.
\end{equation}
On the balanced-growth path output and both capital stocks grow at $g$, while
effective labor is stationary. Taking growth factors gives
\begin{equation}
    g^{1-\alpha-\alpha_G}
    =\left(1+\gamma^{a}\right)^{\vartheta-1}
    \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
    1+\gamma^{a}=g^{(1-\alpha-\alpha_G)/(\vartheta-1)}.
\end{equation}
Private and public capital supply $g^{\alpha+\alpha_G}$ of gross output growth.
The love-of-variety term supplies the remainder. Because $\vartheta-1$ is small,
the technology stock grows faster than output.

\section{Consequence for stationarization}

A single trend at $g$ for every variable would leave production unbalanced: if
$A_t$ also grew at $g$, output would grow at
$g^{\vartheta-1+\alpha+\alpha_G}$ rather than at $g$. The model therefore uses
$g$ for the real block and $1+\gamma^a$ for technology stocks and values. The
adoption law of motion and the value and adoption-effort equations consequently
carry $1+\gamma^a$.

The relation differs from the earlier formula
$g^{(1-\alpha)/(\vartheta-1)}$, which treated public infrastructure as a level
shifter when deriving technology growth. Including $\alpha_G$ is required once
public infrastructure is a proper capital input on the common balanced-growth
path.
