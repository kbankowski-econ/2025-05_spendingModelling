\noindent
The model's equations are the \emph{stationarized} counterparts of the level-form
optimality and accounting conditions. This note works through the transformation for
the private-capital law of motion, from un-detrended (level) variables to the detrended
(stationary) variables the model actually solves. The same logic applies to every
trending equation; the growth factors it produces are exactly the $g$ terms in the
Dynare files.

\section{Start: the level (un-detrended) equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:level}
    K_t = (1-\delta)K_{t-1} + I_t,
\end{equation}
where $K_t$ is the level of private capital and $I_t$ is level investment. Along the
balanced-growth path both grow with the technology trend $\Lambda_t$, at the gross rate
\begin{equation}
    g_t \equiv \frac{\Lambda_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}.
\end{equation}

\section{Define the detrended variables}

Express each quantity relative to the trend, so that it is constant in steady state:
\begin{equation}
    \hat K_t \equiv \frac{K_t}{\Lambda_t}, \qquad
    \hat I_t \equiv \frac{I_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}.
\end{equation}

\section{Stationarize: divide \eqref{eq:level} by $\Lambda_{t-1}$}

$\Lambda_{t-1}$ is the trend attached to the \emph{predetermined} stock $K_{t-1}$, so
dividing the identity by it is the natural choice:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{K_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}
    = (1-\delta)\underbrace{\frac{K_{t-1}}{\Lambda_{t-1}}}_{=\,\hat K_{t-1}}
    + \underbrace{\frac{I_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}}_{=\,\hat I_t}.
\end{equation}
The two right-hand terms are already stationary. The left-hand term is the \emph{new}
stock, one period of growth ``ahead,'' so it carries the growth factor:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{K_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}
    = \frac{K_t}{\Lambda_t}\cdot\frac{\Lambda_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}
    = g_t\,\hat K_t.
\end{equation}

\section{Result: the model equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
    \boxed{\,g_t\,\hat K_t = (1-\delta)\hat K_{t-1} + \hat I_t\,}
\end{equation}
which is exactly the Dynare code \texttt{Kp*g = (1-delta)*Kp(-1) + Ip}.

\section{Where the $g$ comes from}

The factor $g_t$ is nothing but $\Lambda_t/\Lambda_{t-1}$---the \textbf{growth wedge}
between the current stock ($\sim\Lambda_t$) and the predetermined stock and resources it
is built from ($\sim\Lambda_{t-1}$). Detrending changes nothing economic; it simply
relabels every variable relative to the trend, and the leftover $\Lambda$-ratios surface
as the $g$ factors. As a rule:
\begin{itemize}
    \item wherever a level equation compares a date-$t$ quantity with a date-$(t{-}1)$
          one (accumulation identities), a $g_t$ appears on the current variable;
    \item wherever it compares date $t$ with date $t{+}1$ (Euler and asset-pricing
          conditions), a $g_{t+1}$ appears in the discount factor---e.g.\ the bond Euler
          equation $\lambda_t = \beta\,\mathbb{E}_t[\lambda_{t+1}R_t/\Pi_{t+1}]$ becomes
          $\hat\lambda_t = \beta\,\mathbb{E}_t[\hat\lambda_{t+1}R_t/(g_{t+1}\Pi_{t+1})]$,
          because $\lambda$ (marginal utility) falls with the trend and is rescaled
          inversely.
\end{itemize}

\section{Steady-state check}

With $g_t = g$ and $\hat K_t = \hat K_{t-1} = \hat K$, equation~\eqref{eq:model} gives
\begin{equation}
    g\hat K = (1-\delta)\hat K + \hat I
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \hat I = (g-1+\delta)\,\hat K,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ steady-state investment covers \emph{depreciation} ($\delta$) plus \emph{growth of
the stock} ($g-1$). Numerically, $g-1+\delta = 0.004 + 0.025 = 0.029$, and the solved
model has $\hat I/\hat K = 0.1629/5.617 = 0.029$. \checkmark

\section{Why the human-capital stock carries no $g$: stationary vs.\ trending stocks}

The private-capital stock trends, which is why $g_t$ appears in
equation~\eqref{eq:model}. Not every accumulation equation does, though---only those
whose stock \emph{grows} along the balanced-growth path. The human-capital stock is the
telling counterexample. Its law of motion,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hc}
    H_t = (1-\delta^h)H_{t-1}
        + \chi\, E_t^{\gamma}\left(\frac{K^{GE}_{t-1}}{\Lambda_{t-1}}\right)^{\mu},
\end{equation}
relates a stock at $t$ and at $t-1$ in a single equation---yet it carries no growth
factor (in the code, \texttt{H = (1-deltaH)*H(-1) + chiH*\dots}, there is no \texttt{g}).
The reason is that $H_t$ is \emph{stationary}: it does not grow with the trend. Reading
off the right-hand side,
\begin{itemize}
    \item $E_t$, the time devoted to schooling, is a share of the unit time endowment,
          so it is bounded and stationary;
    \item public human-capital capital $K^{GE}_{t-1}$ \emph{does} trend, but it enters
          divided by $\Lambda_{t-1}$---that explicit scaling removes its trend, so the
          whole flow $\chi E_t^{\gamma}(K^{GE}_{t-1}/\Lambda_{t-1})^{\mu}$ is stationary.
\end{itemize}
Hence $H_t$ is an AR(1) in stationary inputs, and is itself stationary (steady state
$H=1$). Both $H_t$ and $H_{t-1}$ are already the same stationary object, so detrending
leaves them untouched and no growth wedge---no $g_t$---appears between them.

The contrast with private capital is exact. There $K_t$ and $K_{t-1}$ trend; expressed
relative to their own-date trends, $\hat K_t = K_t/\Lambda_t$ and
$\hat K_{t-1} = K_{t-1}/\Lambda_{t-1}$ differ by one period of growth, and that ratio
$\Lambda_t/\Lambda_{t-1} = g_t$ is the factor on the current stock in
equation~\eqref{eq:model}. The takeaway: the $g_t$ is triggered not by having stocks at
$t$ and $t-1$ in one equation, but by whether those stocks \emph{grow}. Where a trending
input would otherwise push a stationary stock off its steady level---as $K^{GE}$ would
for $H$---its trend is stripped out by an explicit $\Lambda$ division inside the equation,
which is exactly why that division has to be there.

\section{Where $g$ appears in the model---and why $\Lambda$ does not}

Stationarization divides the trend out of every equation, so \textbf{the trend level
$\Lambda_t$ never appears in the solved model}. The code contains only detrended
variables and growth \emph{rates}; $\Lambda_t$ lives solely in the derivation above, as
the object that is divided away. What remains is the gross growth factor $g_t$, and it
shows up exactly where a level equation compares trending real quantities across dates:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the household Euler equation, \texttt{lambda = betta*(lambda(+1)/g(+1)*R/PI(+1))};
    \item the return on private investment, \texttt{1 = betta*(lambda(+1)/lambda/g(+1)*(1-delta+rk(+1)))};
    \item the three capital laws of motion---private, public infrastructure, and public
          human capital: \texttt{Kp*g = \dots}, \texttt{Kg*g = \dots}, \texttt{Kge*g = \dots};
    \item the government budget, where lagged debt is deflated by growth,
          \texttt{b = (R(-1)/PI)*b(-1)/g + \dots};
    \item and the trend process itself,
          \texttt{log(g) = (1-rho\_g)*log(g(-1)) + rho\_g*log(gss) + epsi\_g}.
\end{itemize}
Everything else is stationary and carries no $g$: consumption, the labor and schooling
first-order conditions, effective labor $N_t = H_{t-1}L_t$, the human-capital stock
(previous section), the Calvo pricing block, the spending instruments, the tax and
transfer rules, and market clearing.

One block runs on a \emph{different} growth rate. The created- and adopted-technology
stocks $Z_t$ and $A_t$ grow at their own gross rate $1+\gamma^a$ (net rate $\gamma^a$;
the model parameter \texttt{gammaa}), not the output rate $g_t$. So the adoption law of
motion, the two value functions, and the adoption first-order condition carry
$1+\gamma^a$ in place of $g_t$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{(1+gammaa)*A = q*phi*(Z(-1)-A(-1)) + phi*A(-1)};
    \item \texttt{V = \dots + phi*SDF(+1)*V(+1)*A(-1)/A/(1+gammaa)}, and likewise for \texttt{J};
    \item \texttt{varsigma*q*phi*SDF(+1)/(1+gammaa)*A(-1)/A*(V(+1)-J(+1)) = S}.
\end{itemize}
In sum, the solved model carries two trend growth \emph{rates}---$g_t$ for the real block
and $1+\gamma^a$ for the technology block---and no trend \emph{level} $\Lambda_t$ at all.

\section{A note on the investment convention}

Above, investment was scaled by the incumbent trend, $\hat I_t = I_t/\Lambda_{t-1}$,
which is what delivers the clean \texttt{+Ip} in the code. Scaling it instead by the
current trend, $I_t/\Lambda_t$, the identical algebra yields
\begin{equation}
    g_t\hat K_t = (1-\delta)\hat K_{t-1} + g_t\hat I_t,
\end{equation}
with the $g_t$ then sitting on the flow as well. This is only a labeling choice for the
investment variable; the substantive content---the $g_t$ on the current stock---is
unchanged.
