\noindent
The model's equations are the \emph{stationarized} counterparts of the level-form
optimality and accounting conditions. This note works through the transformation for
the private-capital law of motion, from un-detrended (level) variables to the detrended
(stationary) variables the model actually solves. The same logic applies to every
trending equation; the growth factors it produces are exactly the $g$ terms in the
Dynare files.

\section{Start: the level (un-detrended) equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:level}
    K_t = (1-\delta)K_{t-1} + I_t,
\end{equation}
where $K_t$ is the level of private capital and $I_t$ is level investment. Along the
balanced-growth path both grow with the technology trend $\Lambda_t$, at the gross rate
\begin{equation}
    g_t \equiv \frac{\Lambda_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}.
\end{equation}

\section{Define the detrended variables}

Express each quantity relative to the trend, so that it is constant in steady state:
\begin{equation}
    \hat K_t \equiv \frac{K_t}{\Lambda_t}, \qquad
    \hat I_t \equiv \frac{I_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}.
\end{equation}

\section{Stationarize: divide \eqref{eq:level} by $\Lambda_{t-1}$}

$\Lambda_{t-1}$ is the trend attached to the \emph{predetermined} stock $K_{t-1}$, so
dividing the identity by it is the natural choice:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{K_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}
    = (1-\delta)\underbrace{\frac{K_{t-1}}{\Lambda_{t-1}}}_{=\,\hat K_{t-1}}
    + \underbrace{\frac{I_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}}_{=\,\hat I_t}.
\end{equation}
The two right-hand terms are already stationary. The left-hand term is the \emph{new}
stock, one period of growth ``ahead,'' so it carries the growth factor:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{K_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}
    = \frac{K_t}{\Lambda_t}\cdot\frac{\Lambda_t}{\Lambda_{t-1}}
    = g_t\,\hat K_t.
\end{equation}

\section{Result: the model equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
    \boxed{\,g_t\,\hat K_t = (1-\delta)\hat K_{t-1} + \hat I_t\,}
\end{equation}
which is exactly the Dynare code \texttt{Kp*g = (1-delta)*Kp(-1) + Ip}.

\section{Where the $g$ comes from}

The factor $g_t$ is nothing but $\Lambda_t/\Lambda_{t-1}$---the \textbf{growth wedge}
between the current stock ($\sim\Lambda_t$) and the predetermined stock and resources it
is built from ($\sim\Lambda_{t-1}$). Detrending changes nothing economic; it simply
relabels every variable relative to the trend, and the leftover $\Lambda$-ratios surface
as the $g$ factors. As a rule:
\begin{itemize}
    \item wherever a level equation compares a date-$t$ quantity with a date-$(t{-}1)$
          one (accumulation identities), a $g_t$ appears on the current variable;
    \item wherever it compares date $t$ with date $t{+}1$ (Euler and asset-pricing
          conditions), a $g_{t+1}$ appears in the discount factor---e.g.\ the bond Euler
          equation $\lambda_t = \beta\,\mathbb{E}_t[\lambda_{t+1}R_t/\Pi_{t+1}]$ becomes
          $\hat\lambda_t = \beta\,\mathbb{E}_t[\hat\lambda_{t+1}R_t/(g_{t+1}\Pi_{t+1})]$,
          because $\lambda$ (marginal utility) falls with the trend and is rescaled
          inversely.
\end{itemize}

\section{Steady-state check}

With $g_t = g$ and $\hat K_t = \hat K_{t-1} = \hat K$, equation~\eqref{eq:model} gives
\begin{equation}
    g\hat K = (1-\delta)\hat K + \hat I
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \hat I = (g-1+\delta)\,\hat K,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ steady-state investment covers \emph{depreciation} ($\delta$) plus \emph{growth of
the stock} ($g-1$). Numerically, $g-1+\delta = 0.004 + 0.025 = 0.029$, and the solved
model has $\hat I/\hat K = 0.1629/5.617 = 0.029$. \checkmark

\section{A note on the investment convention}

Above, investment was scaled by the incumbent trend, $\hat I_t = I_t/\Lambda_{t-1}$,
which is what delivers the clean \texttt{+Ip} in the code. Scaling it instead by the
current trend, $I_t/\Lambda_t$, the identical algebra yields
\begin{equation}
    g_t\hat K_t = (1-\delta)\hat K_{t-1} + g_t\hat I_t,
\end{equation}
with the $g_t$ then sitting on the flow as well. This is only a labeling choice for the
investment variable; the substantive content---the $g_t$ on the current stock---is
unchanged.
